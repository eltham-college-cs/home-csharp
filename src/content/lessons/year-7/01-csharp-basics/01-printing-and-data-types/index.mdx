## The big idea

`Console.WriteLine()` is the computer's **speech bubble**. Whatever you put inside the brackets appears on the screen, on its own line.

Every instruction in C# ends with a **semicolon** `;`, just like a full stop ends a sentence.

```csharp
Console.WriteLine("Hello!");
```

C# has different kinds of data. Today we meet two:

- A **string** is a piece of text. It always wears speech marks: `"Hello"`.
- An **int** (short for integer) is a whole number, like `7`, `42` or `-3`. No speech marks.

<MemoryBoxes boxes={[
  { name: "a string", value: "\"Hello\"" },
  { name: "an int", value: "12" },
  { name: "a string!", value: "\"12\"" }
]} caption='Speech marks make it text. 12 is a number, but "12" is text.' />

You can do maths with ints: `+` add, `-` subtract, `*` multiply, `/` divide.
C# does `*` and `/` before `+` and `-`, just like in maths.
