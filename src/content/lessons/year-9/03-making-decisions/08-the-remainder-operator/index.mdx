<ParentTip>
You don't need to know C#. Use real objects: share 7 sweets between 2 people. How many does each person get, and how many are left over? That is 7 / 2 and 7 % 2.
</ParentTip>

## The big idea

`%` gives you **what is left over** after dividing. Think of sharing sweets fairly:

- 7 sweets between 2 friends: each gets 3, and **1** is left over. So `7 % 2` is `1`.
- 6 sweets between 3 friends: each gets 2, and **0** are left over. So `6 % 3` is `0`.

In C#, `/` and `%` make a perfect pair. When both numbers are ints, `/` gives the whole-number answer and throws the remainder away, and `%` gives you just the remainder:

| Sum | Answer | In sweets |
|---|---|---|
| `17 / 5` | `3` | each of 5 friends gets 3 |
| `17 % 5` | `2` | 2 are left over |

<Note title="Careful with /">
`7 / 2` is `3`, not 3.5, because 7 and 2 are both ints. C# isn't wrong: it's answering "how many whole times?"
</Note>

A remainder of 0 means it divided exactly. That gives us a neat test:

```csharp
if (x % 2 == 0)
{
    Console.WriteLine("even");
}
```
