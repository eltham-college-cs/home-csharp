## The big idea

A **condition** is a yes or no question. C# answers it with `true` or `false`.

| Operator | Means | true when |
|---|---|---|
| `==` | is equal to | `5 == 5` |
| `!=` | is not equal to | `5 != 4` |
| `>` | greater than | `7 > 3` |
| `<` | less than | `3 < 7` |
| `>=` | greater than or equal to | `5 >= 5` |
| `<=` | less than or equal to | `4 <= 5` |

`if`, `else if` and `else` are a **road junction**. C# checks each sign in order and takes the **first** road whose answer is true. Every other road is skipped.

```csharp
if (mark >= 50)
{
    Console.WriteLine("Pass");
}
else
{
    Console.WriteLine("Not yet");
}
```

Two things to notice:

- The condition always goes inside **round brackets** `( )`.
- The lines that belong to each road go inside **curly braces** `{ }`. Braces come in pairs. There is no semicolon after `if (...)` or after a brace.

<Note title="= is not ==">
`mark = 70;` **sets** the box. `mark == 70` **asks** a question. If you write `if (mark = 70)`, C# won't build your program: it says `CS0029: Cannot implicitly convert type 'int' to 'bool'`. That means "I wanted a true or false question, but you gave me a number".
</Note>

## Two questions at once

Sometimes one question isn't enough. You can join two conditions:

- `&&` means **and**: both must be true. `age >= 13 && age <= 19` is true for teenagers.
- `||` means **or**: at least one must be true. `day == "Saturday" || day == "Sunday"` is true at the weekend.
